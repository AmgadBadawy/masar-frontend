# Primary MVP user

## Who they are

The primary MVP user is an Egyptian adult who needs to complete a government procedure for themself or a close family member. They may be a student, employee, parent, job seeker, or older relative's helper. They are not a government-process expert and their need is usually practical and time-sensitive.

## Context

- They often start on a phone, while commuting, at home, or shortly before visiting an authority.
- They arrive with a plain-language need, such as replacing a lost document, rather than an official service name.
- They may have heard conflicting advice from relatives, social media, or search results.
- They need to know what to prepare before spending time and money on a visit.

## Main problems

- They do not know the correct authority or official name of the service.
- Requirements and steps are scattered, unclear, or difficult to verify.
- They cannot easily tell whether a source is official or current.
- They risk wasted visits because a document, condition, or step was missed.

## Goals

- Identify the correct procedure quickly.
- Prepare confidently before visiting or using an official online service.
- Understand the steps in a clear order.
- Reach the official source when they need to verify details or complete the service.

## Frustrations

- Long, jargon-heavy, or contradictory instructions.
- Search results that mix official pages with outdated articles, social posts, or commercial intermediaries.
- Unclear fees, timings, or online availability.
- Having to start the search over when a result is not the right service.

## Technical and digital comfort assumptions

- The user can use a smartphone browser, type or tap a short Arabic search, follow links, and scroll through a short page.
- The user may not know formal government terminology or distinguish domains and organizations reliably.
- The user may have limited bandwidth, a small screen, or a shared device.
- The MVP should not require account creation, app installation, or advanced digital skills.

## What Masar should assume

- The user needs plain Arabic, scannable information, and a clear next step.
- The user benefits from seeing the authority, source, and verification date near the guidance.
- The user may need to compare or backtrack to a similar procedure.

## What Masar should not assume

- That the user knows the exact service name, authority, or required terminology.
- That the user has all documents, reliable internet, or time to read lengthy content.
- That every procedure is available online.
- That an unofficial source is sufficient for a factual claim.
